# TryHabla motion direction

TryHabla should feel lively without making classroom work slower. HablaMan is the visual guide, not decoration pasted onto every surface.

## Teacher surfaces

- Keep navigation immediate and predictable.
- Use short 150–250 ms transitions for page settling, hover, pressed, success, and disclosure states.
- Use HablaMan for onboarding, empty states, important success moments, and occasional contextual help.
- Do not cover grading controls, animate long lists, or delay actions behind a branded transition.

## Student surfaces

- Use stronger 450–800 ms motion for route changes, assignment starts, submissions, and earned progress. It should feel fast while remaining visible enough to read as an intentional transition.
- Base the visual language on microphones, audio waves, speech, and HablaMan—not borrowed game characters or franchise-specific shapes.
- Let HablaMan react to meaningful states: ready, recording, submitted, waiting for feedback, graded, and retry.
- Keep controls readable and usable while the visual layer supplies energy around them.

## Marketing surfaces

- Use scroll-driven scenes to demonstrate the product workflow: assign, record, review, improve.
- Change HablaMan poses only when the story advances rather than keeping him in constant motion.
- Prefer a few memorable transitions over motion on every section.

## Accessibility and performance

- Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and remove all nonessential animation when requested.
- Animate transforms and opacity where possible.
- Do not add artificial loading time. Branded wipes may decorate a real route transition but must not block input or data.
- Keep the canonical HablaMan model, belt buckle, microphone, colors, and proportions consistent across every pose.
